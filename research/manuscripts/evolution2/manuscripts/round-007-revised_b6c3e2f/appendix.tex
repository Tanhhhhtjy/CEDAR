\subsection{Discrete outcomes and continuous geometry}

Every generated answer is classified as \emph{initial}, \emph{induced target}, \emph{other wrong}, or \emph{invalid/abstain}.  This four-way coding prevents a reduction in target following from being misreported as an accuracy gain when probability mass instead moves to another error or an invalid output.  The continuous readout uses the option logits at the frozen answer position:
\begin{align}
m_{\mathrm{general}}
  &= \frac{1}{3}\sum_{j\neq 0}\ell_j-\ell_0,\\
m_{\mathrm{background}}
  &= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\notin\{0,t\}}\ell_j-\ell_0,\\
m_{\mathrm{target}}
  &= \ell_t-\ell_0,\\
m_{\mathrm{specific}}
  &= \ell_t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\notin\{0,t\}}\ell_j.
\end{align}
The identity $m_{\mathrm{target}}=m_{\mathrm{background}}+m_{\mathrm{specific}}$ is checked in every direct-logit record.  A broad reduction of the initial-answer advantage moves $m_{\mathrm{general}}$ and $m_{\mathrm{background}}$; selective attraction toward $a_t$ additionally moves $m_{\mathrm{specific}}$.

\section{Solver-Certified Evidence Data}
\label{supp:data}

\subsection{Generator selection and hardening}

We first compared two zero-GPU construction routes on 16 bases each.  A ProverGen-style route generated a complete Horn world and proof skeleton before naturalization; a second route deterministically searched an atomic-edit neighborhood around an existing certified world.  Both achieved 16/16 valid bases and zero gold errors, but neither alone provided the best combination of controlled initial worlds and certified revision edits.  We therefore froze a hybrid: ProverGen-style complete-world construction followed by deterministic atomic-edit search over candidate facts and rules.

Hardening proceeded before any Qwen run.  Candidate pools contained 20--90 atomic edits; the accepted edit ranked between 2 and 78 under deterministic ordering.  All 48 hardening cases passed unique-gold, atomic-$V$, single-fault-$F$, and countermodel checks.  Two naturalization rounds then removed fixed condition-specific phrases and repaired ambiguous anaphora.  The final construction bank passed its pre-execution audit: 48/48 bases, 288/288 arms, deterministic rebuilding, independent machine validation, frozen-tokenizer checks, and a full human naturalization review.  No model output was consulted while generating, validating, or revising the bank.

\subsection{Symbolic grammar and balancing}

Worlds use finite, function-free Horn rules with explicit negation.  Forward closure is computed by a deterministic Python fixed-point engine.  Prover9 independently checks the queried literal and its explicit opposite.  A base is retained only when both engines agree and the four-way label is unique among \{true, false, unknown, both\}.  Medium variants have strictly greater shortest-proof depth than the easy variant in all 24 families.

\subsection{Object-level and meta-level certification}

Certification is deliberately two-level.  At the object level, the edit must change the answer from $a_0$ to a unique $a_t$.  At the meta level, the argument must or must not warrant applying that edit.  For $V$, Prover9 proves the warrant and the Python engine agrees.  For $F$, one stipulated inference is removed from the valid derivation; all preceding facts and all downstream steps conditional on that inference remain supported.  Mace4 supplies a model in which the stated premises hold and the warrant fails.  The validator also verifies that no second failure point exists.

As a schematic certificate example, one cross-entity-transfer pair observes that an origin check passed for Record~R.  The valid argument applies that result to R and proves the edit criterion.  Its fallacious counterpart changes only the referenced entity: the origin check passed for Record~S but is transferred to R.  Both wrappers propose the same atomic edit and have the same reviewer identity, speech act, and output contract.  The $V$ warrant is proved; the $F$ warrant is neither proved nor refuted by Prover9, and the stored Mace4 countermodel satisfies the observations while making the warrant false.

\subsection{Human review and data scope}

The final 48-base naturalization packet spans 24 structural families, each with an easy and a medium base.  All 48 bases passed semantic fidelity and referential clarity; naturalness was uniformly rated 4/5 because the formal audit register is intentionally more explicit than ordinary conversation.  The review occurred before model execution.  Inter-rater agreement and Cohen's $\kappa$ are unavailable for this bank.

The construction bank comprises 24 structural families with easy and medium variants, for 48 bases in total.  The development Evidence assay selects both variants from 12 families, yielding 24 bases; each selected family is one bootstrap cluster.  Three sampling levels remain distinct throughout the paper.  The reused-item cross-configuration assay holds its 24-base Evidence set fixed across model-specific configurations.  The new-salt assay changes symbolic names, option mappings, and salt within the same 24 family--difficulty cells.  New structural families and natural arguments constitute a separate generalization level.

The 288-record construction packet renders six arm templates per base for data hardening.  The reported development Evidence estimand uses the four $N/R/V/F$ arms, yielding 96 base--arm comparisons per cap condition.  The final Social module is constructed separately from SycoBench-600.

\section{Exposure-Controlled Social Data}
\label{supp:social-data}

\subsection{Source, templates, and balancing}

CEDAR-Social starts from all 600 SycoBench-600 items \citep{sinha2026sycobench}: 272 normalized stems spanning eight domains and three difficulty levels.  The official construction exposes a non-gold target in A or B, so target position is not balanced over all four labels in this module.  Four paired template skeletons are used 150 times each.  The $AO$ template mentions the target without an agent (e.g., ``Option B appears in the choices''); the $S$ counterpart adds first-person confidence and a reconsideration request.  Both prohibit evidence, expertise, authority, reward, threat, and consensus cues.

Automated checks enforce identical base questions, answer pairs, output contracts, and target identities.  The maximum frozen-tokenizer length difference between paired $AO/S$ follow-ups is four tokens.  A 240-item subset, stratified by domain, difficulty, and template, is frozen for free-generation analysis; the direct forced-choice assay uses all 600.

\subsection{Review protocol deviation}

The original protocol requested two independent reviewers.  In the executed study, one data lead reviewed all 120 unique template--stratum pairs before seeing model outputs, and the second review was not obtained.  The single-review deviation was frozen and disclosed before GPU execution.  No agreement statistic or adjudication rate is reported.  This limitation concerns linguistic and construct review; answer identities, pairing, target equality, and token bounds are deterministic and fully audited.

\section{Models, Interventions, and Run Configuration}
\label{supp:protocols}

\subsection{Capping operator}

For layer $l$, released direction $v_l$, threshold $\tau_l$, and residual state $h_l$, p25 applies
\begin{equation}
h_l' = h_l-\left[\langle h_l,v_l\rangle-\tau_l\right]_+v_l,
\end{equation}
with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$.  The operation removes only projection above the threshold and is applied at every eligible token in the specified layer window.  Axis polarity and threshold assets are model-specific; a Qwen direction is never reused on Llama.

\subsection{Compute and software}

The main Qwen generation and phase diagnostics used four NVIDIA RTX~4090 GPUs, Python~3.11, PyTorch~2.6.0+cu124, Transformers~4.56.1, BF16, and SDPA.  Batch size was one, eliminating padding as a cap-exposure confound.  A documented infrastructure continuation changed only visible GPU indices; tokenization, model revision, BF16/SDPA, decoding, seed, and prompts were unchanged, and an anchor-equivalence audit passed 192/192 comparisons.  RG1 reconstructed all treatment and random-reference conditions within one fresh environment and analyzes only within-stack contrasts.  The reused-item Llama audit used the same forced-choice response contract and reports each model on its native logit scale.

\subsection{Run identity, retries, and missing output}

Prompts, rendered token IDs, condition order, model/configuration hashes, and analysis code identity are recorded before every formal run.  Smoke tests precede formal execution.  Content-based retries and answer repair are disabled.  Infrastructure resumption is permitted only from completed immutable records and is separately logged.

All scheduled outputs enter categorical intent-to-analyze summaries.  A truncated or unparsable output becomes \emph{invalid/abstain}.  Continuous analysis requires both relevant option logits.  The new-salt bank has one uncapped $F$ output that reached the token limit and lacks logits; it is retained categorically, yielding 95/96 complete continuous pairs and a formal disposition of \emph{technically inconclusive due to one missing primary pair}.  A post-run tipping analysis shows that imputing the missing change with the worst observed item ($-18.188$ logits) retains a positive aggregate estimate of $+1.331$ ($[0.215,2.591]$); the lower interval reaches zero only below approximately $-29.18$ logits.  This sensitivity describes the incomplete sample and leaves the prespecified completeness criterion unchanged.

\section{Statistical Estimands and Integrity Checks}
\label{supp:inference}

\subsection{Complete estimand definitions}

For outcome $Y$, follow-up condition $x$, and cap configuration $c$, define the paired condition effect
\begin{equation}
\delta_x^Y(c)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid x,c]-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid x,0],
\end{equation}
where $0$ denotes uncapped inference.  The pooled Evidence displacement and continuous/discrete selectivity contrasts are
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\mathrm{broad}}(c)
&=\tfrac{1}{4}\sum_{x\in\{N,R,V,F\}}\delta_x^{m_{\mathrm{general}}}(c),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{sel}}^{(m)}(c)
&=\delta_V^{m_{\mathrm{target}}}(c)-\delta_F^{m_{\mathrm{target}}}(c),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{sel}}^{(p)}(c)
&=[p_V(c)-p_V(0)]-[p_F(c)-p_F(0)].
\end{align}
The pooled change in $m_{\mathrm{specific}}$ is the prespecified target-specificity check; its arm-level decomposition is post hoc.  For Social,
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\mathrm{social}}(c)
&=[S-AO]_c-[S-AO]_0 \nonumber\\
&=\delta_S^{m_{\mathrm{target}}}(c) \nonumber\\
&\quad-\delta_{AO}^{m_{\mathrm{target}}}(c).
\end{align}
The discrete analogue on the frozen free-generation subset is
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{SocialExcess}_q
&=[q_S(p25)-q_{AO}(p25)]\\
&\quad-[q_S(0)-q_{AO}(0)].
\end{align*}

\subsection{Independent units and intervals}

Evidence effects are computed within base and condition, averaged within each structural family, and then averaged with equal family weight.  Unless marked otherwise, 95\% intervals are percentile family-cluster bootstrap intervals with 20,000 draws for the primary Evidence analyses.  Their inferential population is the equally weighted sampled family design.  Leave-one-family-out (LOFO) summaries remove one complete structural family at a time.

Social effects use question-level resampling with domain--difficulty stratification; their primary intervals use 10,000 bootstrap draws (seed 20260723).  The 600 source questions correspond to 272 normalized stems.  The resulting intervals characterize source-question variation; normalized-stem dependence remains unmodeled.  PhilPapers calibration resamples source question identities across the three frozen sample seeds.  The reused-item cross-configuration comparison reports model-native estimates; a cross-model interaction is secondary.  No practical equivalence bound was prespecified for the $V-F$ or cross-model contrasts.  An interval spanning zero therefore leaves the effect unresolved; evidence of preservation would require an equivalence analysis.  The deterministic regime supplies one generation trajectory per item, and intervals quantify item/family variation.

\subsection{Output-space integrity}

Direct-logit records store all four option logits, total probability mass on option labels, full-vocabulary Shannon entropy, and the full-vocabulary top token.  The two summary margins resolve the broad-versus-target-specific partition; redistribution between the two background options remains available in the retained logits.  Free-generation records store finish reason, generated and thinking-token counts, parse status, and the four-way outcome.  Phase and fixed-trace audits additionally bind prefill/decode trigger counts, correction norms, token positions, and cache shape.  No reported primary pattern is produced by selective removal of an invalid output.

\section{Complete Behavioral Results}
\label{supp:behavior}

\subsection{Instrument and operator calibration}

The source-style PhilPapers replication \citep{sharma2024towards} first checked the steering hook, tokenizer, axis sign, and answer-margin implementation.  Across three frozen samples, $+200$ addition reproduced the reported direction with a mean margin change of $-2.431$ logits ($[-2.625,-2.241]$).  The signed curve was not antisymmetric: $-200$ also reduced the margin by $-1.676$ ($[-1.851,-1.497]$).  At these large magnitudes, the vocabulary-wide top token often left the A/B answer space (``Hello'' for 63.9\% at $-200$; ``Based'' for 56.4\% at $+200$), and entropy changed sharply.  Thus a lower within-A/B sycophancy margin alone does not establish a better answer distribution.

Under the released Qwen p25 cap on the same task, every item triggered at least once and 56.66\% of eligible token--layer states triggered.  The mean sycophancy margin fell by $-0.094$ logits ($[-0.133,-0.054]$), but discrete sycophantic choices increased by $+1.44$ percentage points ($[0.11,2.78]$; post hoc).  Flips concentrated near the original decision boundary, whereas high exposure alone did not predict the direction of a flip.

\subsection{Resistance-state hypothesis and trajectory length}

The first telemetry analysis suggested that $S$ items that changed from initial to target had larger uncapped counterfactual correction during decode than stable-initial items: $+3.504$ ($[0.955,5.788]$), especially in layers 50--53.  This motivated a prespecified within-item sampling test of a selective resistance-state account.

The prespecified resist-minus-yield geometry contrast was $-1.146$ ($[-3.294,1.264]$), opposite the predicted sign and crossing zero.  Yield trajectories were much longer than resistance trajectories (1,039 versus 343 thinking tokens).  After length matching, the contrast collapsed to $+0.033$ ($[-1.394,2.166]$), and correction no longer predicted yield once length was controlled.  $F$ provided insufficient within-item variation for a confirmatory estimate.  These results support treating the earlier late-decode association as confounded by trajectory length and composition.

\subsection{Cache-faithful fixed-trace replay}

For trace source $s\in\{U,P\}$, where $U$ and $P$ denote prefixes generated uncapped and under p25, the fixed-trace estimands are
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\mathrm{FT}}(s)
&=\mathbb{E}\!\left[m_{\mathrm{general}}(p25;r_s)-m_{\mathrm{general}}(0;r_s)\right],\\
I_{\mathrm{FT}}&=\Delta_{\mathrm{FT}}(P)-\Delta_{\mathrm{FT}}(U).
\end{align}
Every visible token is fixed, so the comparison estimates an answer-boundary effect conditional on a trace.  Natural-generation total effects come from the generation assay.

For each of 96 prompts, we freeze the visible reasoning tokens generated under uncapped and p25 inference.  Each token sequence is then replayed under both forward conditions, yielding a crossed $2\times2$ design.  The replay path uses the same incremental KV-cache route as generation; it fixes token identity, position, attention context, and the answer-boundary readout.  The initial whole-prefix BF16 implementation failed a $10^{-4}$ replay-fidelity gate because its numerical path differed from incremental cached generation.  A code-only amendment replaced it with cache-faithful forced tokens.  The final replay gate passed 192/192 source traces; no nonfinite values or hook leaks occurred.

\subsection{Downstream displacement composition and auxiliary validity probe}

Let $\operatorname{OffShare}_l$ be the fraction of paired hidden-state displacement energy orthogonal to the fixed layer-53 exit direction at downstream layer $l$.  The prespecified propagation statistic is
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{off}}=\operatorname{OffShare}_{60}-\operatorname{OffShare}_{53}.
\end{equation}
The capped window itself uses layer-specific directions; layer 53 is therefore the exit reference for the downstream composition measurement.

On uncapped-source traces, hidden-state displacement is measured at layers 45--63.  Layers 46--53 use the released layer-specific axis; layers 54--63 project onto the fixed layer-53 exit direction.  Four schedule-, token-, and norm-matched random-direction interventions form controlled references.

The prespecified $G_{\mathrm{off}}$ statistic is the change in off-exit-axis energy share from layer 53 to 60.  The p25 estimate is $0.1608$ ($[0.1510,0.1701]$); 12/12 family effects are positive and LOFO estimates range from $0.1584$ to $0.1635$.  Arm estimates are $N=0.145$, $R=0.142$, $V=0.184$, and $F=0.172$.  All four matched random references are within $7\times10^{-5}$ of zero.  Within the RG1 stack, $\Delta m_{\mathrm{general}}=+1.339$ ($[0.911,1.754]$).  Family-level $G_{\mathrm{off}}$ and margin change correlate at $\rho=-0.175$; with only 12 clusters, this relationship is descriptive.

An auxiliary family-held-out linear probe at layer 60 decodes $V/F$ validity under both conditions: ROC--AUC is 0.769 uncapped and 0.767 under p25; balanced accuracy is 0.688 and 0.708.  A text-only TF--IDF baseline is at chance (AUC 0.511; balanced accuracy 0.500).  Family-label permutation yields $p=0.014$ and $0.002$ for the neural probes and $0.94$ for TF--IDF.  The probe supports condition-wise linear decodability; cross-condition transfer and causal intervention remain separate endpoints.

\subsection{Boundary analysis}

We also tested whether absolute uncapped margin universally predicts a paired choice flip.  A pooled clustered logistic analysis gave a negative slope of $-0.923$ ($[-1.457,-0.588]$), but the relation was driven by forced-choice PhilPapers.  Removing that dataset changed the slope to $+0.564$, the development Evidence assay moved in the opposite direction, and CEDAR-Social produced only one free-generation flip among 480 paired runs.  The boundary account is informative for forced-choice flips; its direction does not generalize across these datasets.  The main paper therefore relates discrete expression to the surrounding decision geometry and limits the absolute-margin account to the observed forced-choice setting.

\section{Prospective Controller Evaluation}
\label{supp:controller}

The account in the main paper yields a prospective comparison between controllers that separate drift detection from the actuation applied after a trigger.  Let $u$ be the released actuation direction and let $\Pi_E$ project onto an evidence-sensitive subspace estimated only from controller-development families.  A protected direction is
\begin{equation}
u_{\perp}=\frac{(I-\Pi_E)u}{\lVert(I-\Pi_E)u\rVert_2}.
\end{equation}
This construction states a testable design principle: protect components shown to support warranted revision while operating on a learned evidence-sensitive subspace.

A concrete comparison ladder separates three choices.  L0 is the released one-sided threshold cap.  L1 retains the released trigger but redirects the corrective update onto $u_{\perp}$, separating detection from actuation.  L2 uses the protected decomposition for both gating and actuation, so evidence-sensitive movement alone cannot satisfy the removal rule.  Subspace estimation, thresholds, layers, and matching tolerances must be selected only on controller-development families and frozen before held-out evaluation.

The comparison begins by matching independently measured persona benefit, which is a separate endpoint from the present reason-revision assays.  At matched operating points, valid updating should be preserved, fallacious updating should not increase, target-hidden broad movement should shrink, and output integrity, exposure, capability, and compute should remain acceptable.  The present results define this evaluation contract; the controller variants and benefit-matched comparison remain to be run.

We propose cross-condition probe transfer and bidirectional activation patching as direct mechanism tests.  Transfer would ask whether capped and uncapped inference share a usable validity code; patching would ask whether that code causally participates in revision.  New structural families, natural argument datasets, and additional released model--axis pairs would test generalization beyond the present grammar and configurations.

\subsection{Checklist-facing reproducibility details}

The planned release package will include: (i) machine-readable train-free data-generation and validation code; (ii) all prompts and response contracts; (iii) model and tokenizer revisions; (iv) model-specific axis/configuration hashes; (v) solver versions and proof/countermodel traces; (vi) software and hardware manifests; (vii) smoke and formal run commands; (viii) row-level results, finish reasons, parse status, and logits; (ix) bootstrap seeds and complete estimate ledgers; and (x) deterministic delivery manifests. The release package will exclude author names, usernames, repository URLs, machine paths, and Git metadata.

\subsection{Claim-status boundaries}

The evidence hierarchy separates sampling levels.  Development Evidence is exploratory.  The new-salt assay samples new instances from the same families and has one missing continuous pair.  The reused-item Qwen--Llama comparison holds inputs fixed while changing the bundled model--axis configuration.  New structural families and natural arguments remain a separate generalization endpoint.  CEDAR-Social has deterministic pairing audits and one human reviewer.  Fixed-trace replay is a conditional continuous assay and produces no discrete answer changes.  RG1 measures relative downstream composition; the auxiliary probe measures condition-wise decodability.  The protected-controller ladder is a prospective evaluation contract.  These roles are encoded in the claim register and preserved in the manuscript wording.
